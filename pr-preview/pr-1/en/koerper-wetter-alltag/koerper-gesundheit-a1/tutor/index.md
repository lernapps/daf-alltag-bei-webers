## Context: the page “Body Parts and Basic Health”

Name basic parts of the body and say in German when something hurts or you feel sick.

### Peter Catches a Cold

In autumn, Peter catches a cold in Lindenberg. On the **health check** of his board, he writes down key body parts and two patterns to say something hurts: **Mein Kopf tut weh** (singular) / **Meine Ohren tun weh** (plural), or **Ich habe Kopfschmerzen** (I have a headache).

Vocabulary (German – English): Body parts and aches
- der Kopf – head. Example: Mein Kopf tut weh. – My head hurts.
- der Hals – throat / neck. Example: Ich habe Halsschmerzen. – I have a sore throat.
- das Auge / das Ohr, \"die Augen / die Ohren\" – eye / ear
- die Nase / der Mund – nose / mouth
- der Bauch / der Rücken – stomach / back
- der Arm / die Hand, \"die Arme / die Hände\" – arm / hand
- das Bein / der Fuß, \"die Beine / die Füße\" – leg / foot
- krank / gesund – sick / healthy. Example: Ich bin krank und habe Fieber. – I am sick and have a fever.
- Gute Besserung! – Get well soon!

### Exercise koerperteile-zuordnen

- Task: Match the German body parts to their English meanings.
- Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
- Solution: der Hals – throat / neck; der Bauch – stomach; der Rücken – back; das Bein – leg
- Explanation: With these body parts plus '-schmerzen', you can name almost any ache: Halsschmerzen, Bauchschmerzen, Rückenschmerzen.

### Exercise schmerzen-sagen

- Task: Choose the right word for Peter's conversation with Frau Weber.
- Answer format: choose a word for each gap
- How the app counts mistakes of form: capital letters wrong, accents wrong, hyphen wrong, apostrophe wrong, punctuation wrong
- Items (start → solution):
1. My head hurts. → Mein Kopf tut weh.
2. My legs hurt after soccer. → Meine Beine tun weh.
3. I am sick today. → Ich bin heute krank.
4. Frau Weber says: Get well soon! → Frau Weber sagt: Gute Besserung!
- Explanation: One body part (singular) takes 'tut weh'; multiple body parts (plural) take 'tun weh'. When someone is sick, you wish them 'Gute Besserung!'.
- How to help: the app checks word by word against the solutions above and names mistakes of form (e.g. accents) with the child's words. Do not give the solution: ask about the rule (ending, gender, tense) or about a similar sentence of the page. If the app takes a version of the child's as wrong that you think is right, tell the child: the app knows only the versions above.
