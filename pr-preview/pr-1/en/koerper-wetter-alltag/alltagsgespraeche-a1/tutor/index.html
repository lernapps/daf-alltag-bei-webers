Special requirements

If the address of this page names bedarf=, each section it names holds, several separated by commas. It goes before what is said above; if two contradict each other, the stricter one holds. Without bedarf= none holds.

  • kurze-schritte: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, one thought per message, each part of a section with a question of its own. Say how far there is still to go.
  • wenig-lesen: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, without subordinate clauses; say the core of a section yourself instead of having it read. Explain every technical term once, do not correct spelling.
  • mehr-tiefe: Tick off sections the child knows with one checking question. Unlike above you may ask why and what-if questions about the page's examples; but do not make up tasks to work out.
  • andere-muttersprache: Answer in simple language, in the language of this page; give a new technical term with its article and plural, if any, and, if you know it for sure, its translation in brackets. If the child does not understand twice, explain one sentence in its language.
  • klar-und-woertlich: Write literally: no irony, no idioms. Unlike above, at the start you ask with two or three answers to choose from, and you always say “section x of y”.
  • rechnen-anschaulich: Show numbers of the page as amounts, e.g. “37 is 3 tens and 7 ones”, but never the result of a task. One step per message, never time pressure.

Context: the page “Simple Everyday Dialogues”

Manage short everyday dialogues such as ordering food, shopping, and asking simple questions.

Three Steps for Any Short Dialogue

In Lindenberg, Sam handles short conversations at the bakery, café, or on the street using the same three-step pattern: 1. Greet, 2. State your request or question, 3. Pay and say goodbye.

Example: Mini-Dialogue at Café Lindenberg

  • Server: Guten Tag! Was möchten Sie gern? (Good day! What would you like?)
  • Sam: Guten Tag! Ich hätte gern einen Apfelsaft und ein Stück Käsekuchen, bitte. (I would like an apple juice and a slice of cheesecake, please.)
  • Server: Gern! Sonst noch etwas? (Gladly! Anything else?)
  • Sam: Nein, danke. Was kostet das zusammen? (No, thank you. How much is that together?)
  • Server: Das macht 6,80 €. (That comes to €6.80.)
  • Sam: Hier sind 7,50 €, stimmt so. Vielen Dank und auf Wiedersehen! (Here is €7.50, keep the change. Thank you and goodbye!)

Remember: When You Do Not Understand

Two sentences save any A1 dialogue: „Entschuldigung, können Sie das bitte wiederholen?“ (Could you please repeat that?) and „Sprechen Sie bitte etwas langsamer?“ (Could you please speak a bit more slowly?).

Exercise dialog-reihenfolge

  • Task: Put this short bakery dialogue in the right order.
  • Answer format: put the items in the right order (the app shows them shuffled)
  • Solution: 1. Sam: Guten Morgen! Ich hätte gern zwei Brötchen, bitte., 2. Verkäuferin: Gern! Sonst noch etwas?, 3. Sam: Nein, danke, das ist alles. Was kostet das?, 4. Verkäuferin: Das macht 1,20 €, bitte., 5. Sam: Hier, bitte schön. Danke und tschüss!
  • Explanation: An everyday dialogue follows a clear flow: greeting and request → follow-up question → closing and price question → total → payment and goodbye.

Exercise nachfragen-dialog

  • Task: The pharmacist speaks very quickly and Sam misses the price. What is the best thing to say?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ Entschuldigung, können Sie das bitte noch einmal langsamer sagen? – Polite, clear, and effective: you ask for a slower repetition.
    • ✗ Guten Appetit! Ich habe zwei Geschwister. – That fits neither the pharmacy context nor your question about the price.
    • ✗ Tschüss, das macht nichts! – That ends the conversation without finding out the price.
  • Solution: Entschuldigung, können Sie das bitte noch einmal langsamer sagen?
  • Explanation: With 'Entschuldigung, können Sie das bitte wiederholen / langsamer sagen?', you stay in control of any everyday conversation.
