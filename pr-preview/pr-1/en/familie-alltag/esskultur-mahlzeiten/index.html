Breakfast, Lunch, and Abendbrot

Peter is surprised on his first Sunday at the Webers': the warm main meal is often eaten at lunchtime, while dinner is traditionally a cold Abendbrot — bread or rolls with cheese, cold cuts, and sliced vegetables. In the afternoon, the Webers sometimes have Kaffee und Kuchen (coffee and cake).

Typical meals and dining customs in Germany
OccasionTypical in Germany
Breakfast (approx. 7–9 a.m.)Bread rolls with butter, jam or cheese, muesli, coffee or tea.
Lunch (approx. 12–2 p.m.)Traditionally the warm main meal (at the school cafeteria, canteen, or home).
Abendbrot / Dinner (approx. 6–8 p.m.)Often cold bread with toppings, salad, or soup; today also often warm.
Restaurant & CaféPeople say 'Guten Appetit!', pay at the table, and usually round up about 5–10% as a tip.

Paying at a Restaurant: Together or Separate?

The server often asks: „Zusammen oder getrennt?“ (Together or separate?). If the bill is €18.40 and you hand over €20, you say directly when paying: „Machen Sie zwanzig, bitte.“ (Make it twenty) or „Stimmt so.“ (Keep the change) — people rarely leave a tip on the table afterward.

Which statements about food culture in Germany are true?

  • Before eating, people wish each other „Guten Appetit!“. right answer

    Correct! „Guten Appetit!“ is the standard phrase before meals.

  • „Abendbrot“ traditionally means a cold evening meal with bread and toppings. right answer

    True! Many families eat a warm lunch and bread with cheese, cold cuts, or vegetables in the evening.

  • You are never allowed to pay separately in a restaurant.

    On the contrary: paying separately („Getrennt, bitte“) is completely normal in Germany.

  • „Kaffee und Kuchen“ is a typical afternoon tradition, especially on weekends. right answer

    Exactly, often between 3 and 5 p.m. with family or friends.

Saying Guten Appetit, cold Abendbrot, afternoon coffee and cake, and separate bills are all everyday German customs.
