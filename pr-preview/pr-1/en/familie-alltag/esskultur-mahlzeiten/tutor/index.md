## Context: the page “Food Culture and Meals”

Describe typical meal times, bakeries, cafés, and restaurant customs such as tipping and paying in German-speaking countries.

### Breakfast, Lunch, and Abendbrot

Sam is surprised on his first Sunday at the Webers': the warm main meal is often eaten at **lunchtime**, while dinner is traditionally a cold **Abendbrot** — bread or rolls with cheese, cold cuts, and sliced vegetables. In the afternoon, the Webers sometimes have **Kaffee und Kuchen** (coffee and cake).

Typical meals and dining customs in Germany:
- Occasion | Typical in Germany
- Breakfast (approx. 7–9 a.m.) | Bread rolls with butter, jam or cheese, muesli, coffee or tea.
- Lunch (approx. 12–2 p.m.) | Traditionally the warm main meal (at the school cafeteria, canteen, or home).
- Abendbrot / Dinner (approx. 6–8 p.m.) | Often cold bread with toppings, salad, or soup; today also often warm.
- Restaurant & Café | People say 'Guten Appetit!', pay at the table, and usually round up about 5–10% as a tip.

**Remember: Paying at a Restaurant: Together or Separate?**

The server often asks: **„Zusammen oder getrennt?“** (*Together or separate?*). If the bill is €18.40 and you hand over €20, you say directly when paying: **„Machen Sie zwanzig, bitte.“** (*Make it twenty*) or **„Stimmt so.“** (*Keep the change*) — people rarely leave a tip on the table afterward.

### Exercise trinkgeld-verhalten

- Task: Sam and Herr Weber eat at a café in Lindenberg. The bill is €13.50. Sam wants to pay €15 including tip. What does he say to the server?
- Answer format: choose one answer
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ „Fünfzehn Euro, bitte.“ (handing over the bill directly when paying) – That is the standard custom in Germany: state the new total directly as you pay.
  - ✗ He pays exactly €13.50 and silently leaves coins on the table afterward. – In Germany, you round up directly with the server rather than leaving coins silently on the table.
  - ✗ He tips €25, because 100% is customary in Germany. – Customary tipping in Germany is around 5–10% (or rounding up to the next clean euro for small bills).
- Solution: „Fünfzehn Euro, bitte.“ (handing over the bill directly when paying)
- Explanation: When paying in German-speaking countries, you state the rounded-up total directly (about 5–10% tip).

### Exercise esskultur-fakten

- Task: Which statements about food culture in Germany are true?
- Answer format: choose all right answers
- Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
  - ✓ Before eating, people wish each other „Guten Appetit!“. – Correct! „Guten Appetit!“ is the standard phrase before meals.
  - ✓ „Abendbrot“ traditionally means a cold evening meal with bread and toppings. – True! Many families eat a warm lunch and bread with cheese, cold cuts, or vegetables in the evening.
  - ✗ You are never allowed to pay separately in a restaurant. – On the contrary: paying separately („Getrennt, bitte“) is completely normal in Germany.
  - ✓ „Kaffee und Kuchen“ is a typical afternoon tradition, especially on weekends. – Exactly, often between 3 and 5 p.m. with family or friends.
- Solution: Before eating, people wish each other „Guten Appetit!“.; „Abendbrot“ traditionally means a cold evening meal with bread and toppings.; „Kaffee und Kuchen“ is a typical afternoon tradition, especially on weekends.
- Explanation: Saying Guten Appetit, cold Abendbrot, afternoon coffee and cake, and separate bills are all everyday German customs.
