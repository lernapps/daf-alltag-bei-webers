Special requirements

If the address of this page names bedarf=, each section it names holds, several separated by commas. It goes before what is said above; if two contradict each other, the stricter one holds. Without bedarf= none holds.

  • kurze-schritte: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, one thought per message, each part of a section with a question of its own. Say how far there is still to go.
  • wenig-lesen: Unlike above: two short sentences at most, without subordinate clauses; say the core of a section yourself instead of having it read. Explain every technical term once, do not correct spelling.
  • mehr-tiefe: Tick off sections the child knows with one checking question. Unlike above you may ask why and what-if questions about the page's examples; but do not make up tasks to work out.
  • andere-muttersprache: Answer in simple language, in the language of this page; give a new technical term with its article and plural, if any, and, if you know it for sure, its translation in brackets. If the child does not understand twice, explain one sentence in its language.
  • klar-und-woertlich: Write literally: no irony, no idioms. Unlike above, at the start you ask with two or three answers to choose from, and you always say “section x of y”.
  • rechnen-anschaulich: Show numbers of the page as amounts, e.g. “37 is 3 tens and 7 ones”, but never the result of a task. One step per message, never time pressure.

Context: the page “Doctor Visits and Health”

Describe symptoms, understand simple advice at the doctor's or pharmacy, and talk about staying healthy.

At the Family Doctor's and the Pharmacy

Peter's cold gets worse: he has a Husten (cough), Schnupfen (runny nose), and a slight Fieber (fever). Frau Weber calls the Arztpraxis (doctor's office) to book a Termin (appointment). Peter brings his Versichertenkarte (health insurance card), then picks up his medication with a Rezept (prescription) at the Apotheke (pharmacy).

Vocabulary (German – English): Symptoms, doctor's visit, and pharmacy

  • der Termin – appointment. Example: Ich brauche bitte einen Termin bei Frau Dr. Klein. – I need an appointment with Dr. Klein, please.
  • die Versichertenkarte – health insurance card
  • der Husten / der Schnupfen / das Fieber – cough / runny nose / fever
  • das Rezept – prescription. Note: In German, 'das Rezept' means both a cooking recipe and a doctor's prescription.
  • die Apotheke – pharmacy
  • die Tablette / der Hustensaft – tablet (pill) / cough syrup
  • die Krankschreibung – sick note (for school or work)
  • sich ausruhen / viel trinken – to rest / to drink plenty of fluids

Example: Understanding the Doctor's Advice

  • „Nehmen Sie die Tabletten dreimal täglich nach dem Essen.“ (Take the tablets three times a day after meals.)
  • „Bleiben Sie drei Tage im Bett und trinken Sie viel Tee.“ (Stay in bed for three days and drink lots of tea.)
  • „Hier ist Ihre Krankschreibung für die Schule.“ (Here is your sick note for school.)

Exercise arzt-begriffe

  • Task: Match each German term to the right situation in Germany.
  • Answer format: match each item on the left with one on the right
  • Solution: die Versichertenkarte – shown at the reception desk of the doctor's office; das Rezept – needed at the pharmacy for prescription medication; die Krankschreibung – excuses absence from school or work; der Hustensaft – helps with a bad cough
  • Explanation: In Germany, Termin, Versichertenkarte, Rezept, and Krankschreibung are standard parts of visiting a doctor.

Exercise dosierung-verstehen

  • Task: Peter's medication label says: „Zweimal täglich eine Tablette vor dem Essen.“ What should Peter do?
  • Answer format: choose one answer
  • Choices (✓ right, ✗ wrong, with the app's message):
    • ✓ He takes one tablet twice a day, before eating. – Exactly: 'zweimal täglich' = twice a day, 'vor dem Essen' = before meals.
    • ✗ He takes two tablets at once after dinner. – 'zweimal täglich eine Tablette' means one tablet at two different times of day, and 'vor' means before.
    • ✗ He takes only one tablet per week. – 'täglich' means daily, not weekly.
  • Solution: He takes one tablet twice a day, before eating.
  • Explanation: Key dosage phrases are 'einmal / zweimal / dreimal täglich' (1x/2x/3x daily) and 'vor / nach dem Essen' (before / after meals).
